# 005 Combat and rosters (milestone 5)

- **CMB-1**: Damage is the weapon's base damage times the damage-type percentage against the target's armor class, rounded down, never below 1.
- **CMB-2**: A weapon can hit air targets only if it is anti-air and ground targets only if it is anti-ground.
- **CMB-3**: A shooter fires only at targets within weapon range and never faster than the weapon cooldown.
- **CMB-4**: Idle units and defences automatically acquire visible enemies in range; a unit on a plain move order neither fires nor diverts.
- **CMB-5**: An attack order chases and kills its target; enemies on tiles the owner cannot see cannot be targeted.
- **CMB-6**: An attack-move engages enemies met on the way and then continues to its destination.
- **CMB-7**: Projectiles with flight time land later; splash damage hits enemy ground units near the impact point with falloff and spares friendly units.
- **CMB-8**: Units at zero hit points are removed and their attackers drop them; destroyed buildings free their tiles.
- **CMB-9**: Defence structures fire automatically and go offline while their owner is on low power.
- **CMB-10**: Units with regeneration heal when they have not taken damage for five seconds.
- **CMB-11**: Air units fly over blocked terrain and do not collide with ground units.
- **CMB-12**: A player with no buildings is defeated; the last player standing wins; the snapshot reports when the game is over and who won.
- **CMB-13**: A superweapon charges only while powered, fires at a chosen point after a delay, damages everything in its radius with falloff, and then recharges.
- **ROS-1**: Every building and unit of both factions is reachable through the tech tree from the headquarters, and all prerequisites and producers exist.
- **ROS-2**: Each faction can counter armour, infantry and air: it has an anti-armour weapon, an anti-air weapon, and artillery that outranges its own defences.
- **ROS-3**: At equal cost, like-for-like fights between the factions (rifle infantry, scouts, anti-armour infantry, tanks, and a mixed early-to-mid army) end within 20% of even on open ground, averaged over two army sizes and both starting sides; no faction out-trades the other across its core roster.
- **ROS-4**: In full matches between equal hard AIs on every map, from both starting sides, neither faction wins more than three quarters of the decided games. The Vrax anti-armour Spitter has the same range and training time as the Aegis Rocketeer, so it no longer outranges the Aegis army.
- **CMB-14**: Units cannot fire through buildings: a direct-fire shot needs a line to its target that crosses no building other than the target itself; a unit with its target in range but blocked moves for a clear shot, and while picking targets it passes over blocked ones. Lobbed weapons (artillery), aircraft, and shots at aircraft fire over buildings. Turrets are unaffected.
- **HERO-1**: The Aegis hero, the Paladin, replaces the Aegis Gunship: a giant four-legged assault walker built at the Motor Pool once a Tech Lab stands, with 4500 hit points (more than eight Bulwark Tanks; BAL-15), that repairs itself out of combat and whose twin cannons and missile pods hit both ground and air. A player can have only one at a time, alive or in production: the sidebar shows "Only one at a time" while it is taken, the simulation refuses a second, and the AI fields one whenever it can afford it, never two. Its 3D model is about twice the scale of a tank and long and low (overall at least 1.4 times as long as it is tall, its legs shorter than its hull is long), carried on four thick reverse-jointed legs (each at least 0.25 tiles across before scaling, thicker than its cannon barrels) stepping in a smooth diagonal trot (a long stride, feet that lift and set down gently), with gold trim, a glowing visor, a missile pod on each shoulder and twin cannons beneath that turn to its target. Its cannons fire with a heavy recoil, a large flash with side jets from the muzzle brakes, a pressure ring and a rolling cloud of gun smoke, and their shells land in a bigger burst with a ring of dust and thrown dirt than an ordinary cannon's, still dimmer than a death (R3D-12).
- **HERO-2**: The Vrax hero, the Dreadstrider, replaces the Wasp: a towering six-legged bio-walker built at the Brood Pit once an Evolution Chamber stands, a match for the Paladin in toughness (4500 hit points; BAL-15) and firepower, that regenerates and whose underslung acid cannon hits ground and air. Like the Paladin it is one at a time, alive or in production, for players and the AI alike. With both heroes in, neither faction fields aircraft; the rules for aircraft stay in force for any that may come. Its 3D model is a carapace carried high on six long, splayed, jointed legs, with bone plates and spines along its back, glowing acid vents, a low armoured head and an acid cannon on a swivelling mount beneath it.
- **HERO-3**: A hero dies dramatically, blown apart rather than toppling: it shudders as internal blasts ripple through it for over a second, and with each blast one of its parts (legs, guns, missile pods) is thrown out and falls; then a great final blast throws two shockwaves and a shower of debris and heaves the hull up and down last. Each part flies outward from where it sat, lands, bounces once and comes to rest without sinking into the ground; the parts blacken as they burn, the wreck burns and smokes for several seconds, the camera shakes hard, and the pieces sink away at the end. A Vrax hero bursts in acid and ichor rather than fire.
- **HERO-4**: Heroes fire slow, heavy shots: at most one every 1.5 s, each at least twice a Bulwark Tank shell, bursting over an area that hurts every enemy ground unit near the target (an aircraft it is aimed at is still hit), with sustained firepower close to the old weapon's (at least 85% of it, since BAL-15 made heroes tanks rather than damage dealers). The shot has its own animation: the hero's whole body heaves with it (the Paladin rocks back on its legs, the Dreadstrider lunges into its spit), the Paladin's shell flies flat and straight with a smoke trail (BAL-4) and lands in a great burst with a shockwave, and the Dreadstrider sprays acid from its maw and fires a straight acid bolt that lands in a wide splash with a green shock ring.
- **HERO-5**: Heroes turn slowly and shoot only where they face. The simulation keeps each hero's facing (256 steps a circle) and turns it at most 2 steps a tick (about 56 degrees a second, a half turn in over 3 s): toward its next waypoint while it has somewhere to go, else toward its target. A hero walks only once it faces within 22.5 degrees of its way, so it turns in place before setting off in a new direction, and it fires only at a target within about 11 degrees of its facing (the reach of its guns' own swivel), on the move too. A hero is never pushed by a smaller unit, friend or foe (they give way instead), and does not step aside for allies (SIM-9). The 3D view draws the hero's body turned exactly as the simulation faces it. Facing is integer only, so every browser agrees.
- **HERO-6**: The Paladin walks smoothly (#114). Each leg is a thigh, a shin and a foot that bend at a reverse knee, posed so the foot lands where it should: a foot on the ground stays put on the ground while the hull moves over it (it slides less than 0.02 tiles over a step at a steady walk), then lifts and swings forward to plant again, and lies level on the ground. Its hull bobs (at most about 0.03 tiles) and sways gently and smoothly in time with the steps, its hips riding with it. Its walk eases in and out as it starts and stops, over about a second, rather than snapping between walking and standing. This is drawing only and changes nothing in the simulation; other walkers, the Dreadstrider among them, walk as before.
- **HERO-7**: The Paladin has a second weapon, its shoulder missile pods (#128), beside the cannon (which keeps its numbers; hit points stay 4500, BAL-15). Every 4 s (80 ticks) the pods fire a salvo of 4 missiles at an enemy within 9 tiles that they can hit, ground or air; the missiles leave 3 ticks apart, fly 8 ticks and each does 10 rocket damage with a 1-tile splash (BAL-3 armour scaling, less towards the edge, ground units only, the aimed aircraft always) when it lands, not when it is launched. Issue #128 first asked for 30 per missile; the number is 10 because heavier pods broke the balance checks: a lone Paladin outlasted eighteen Spitter squads (BAL-15), and at 15 the Doomsday AI won only 2 of 8 games against Balanced (AI-15), which it wins with the pods off or at 10. Like the cannon, the pods fire only at a target the hero faces (HERO-5), and the missiles arc over buildings. The simulation keeps the pod's own cooldown (`cooldown2`, in the state hash) and the shot events carry the missile's launch delay (`delay`, additive); the network version is bumped to 6. In the 3D view the pods swing open (mouths tilted up) just before the first missile leaves and fold shut after the last, each missile arcs out of a pod with a smoke trail, and its landing bursts with a flash; pod pose and missile positions are pure functions of the time since the salvo (the classic view shows the same missiles as arcing shots, a few ticks apart).
