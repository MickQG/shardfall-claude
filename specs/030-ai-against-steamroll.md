# 030 The AI against a steamroll (milestone 30)

A competent player beat the Hard AI by default with a steamroll: massing crushers (Bulwark Tanks, Brutes, the hero), driving them forward as one body and flattening the AI's infantry squads, which removed most of its damage. Measured in the open-ground battle harness at equal cost, a pure crusher stack beat the army the AI built against it in nearly every pairing, because the AI answered armour with anti-armour squads that crushers run over. The units that beat a steamroll already exist (tanks with artillery, the hero), so the fix is in what the AI builds and how it moves; unit stats and the crush rule are unchanged, and Difficulty still changes only reaction time, orders per decision and shard bonus (AI-10). Issues #174 to #177.

- **HAR-5**: The harness has a steamroll opponent, for measuring only: it is not a personality, Random never draws it and the menu does not offer it. It runs the AI's own economy and base building at Hard's reaction time and orders per decision with no shard bonus (a human player gets none). It trains only its faction's crusher (Bulwark Tank or Brute) and, as soon as it can, its hero; it builds one barracks (the tech tree needs it), factories where the AI would build further barracks, a lab for its hero and no superweapon. It attacks as one body with crush orders (AI-11, BAL-5) and never retreats a wave. The same seeds and settings give the same game (HAR-1), and an AI that does not face it plays exactly as before. A match series plays the Hard AI of each personality against it (8 seeds, Aegis against Vrax both ways round, from both sides).
- **AI-21**: Every AI keeps a standing scout, so it knows what the enemy is building. Once the building that trains its scout unit stands (Scout Buggy at the Vehicle Factory, Stalker at the Brood Pit), it trains one, ahead of other combat units from that building, and trains a replacement when it has none (not within 30 s of losing one, so it does not feed scouts to the same guns). The standing scout is never part of the army: it joins no wave, gathering or base defence, and the AI trains no second scout unit while it lives. It watches rather than fights: it tours observation points, in turn the ground midway between the two bases and points around the enemy base about 9 tiles out from the enemy buildings it knows (outside the range of every gun but artillery and the Paladin's missile pods), moving on when it reaches one; and it backs away from any armed enemy unit or defence that comes within 8 tiles of it, whatever its health, to the nearest point of its tour (or its own staging point) that has no armed enemy within 8 tiles. Until it has a scout unit the AI scouts as before with its fastest unit (AI-17), which then rejoins the army; every AI still finds an enemy building by minute 4 (AI-17), and Raider still scouts first (AI-14). Difficulty changes none of this beyond reaction time and orders per decision (AI-10). Measured in headless Medium-against-Medium matches on every map, from both sides: the scout survives, and the AI's knowledge of the enemy army stays fresh (thresholds set from the baseline in issue #175).
