# 030 The AI against a steamroll (milestone 30)

A competent player beat the Hard AI by default with a steamroll: out-producing it (three shard fields with three or four haulers each, a chain of buildings toward the middle fields, four or five barracks and as many factories), massing rifle and anti-armour squads around crushers (Bulwark Tanks or Brutes), and winning with one attack in which the crushers flatten the AI's infantry squads. The AI fed that army with early waves, stopped at eight haulers, and answered armour with anti-armour squads that crushers run over: at equal cost in the open-ground battle harness a crusher stack beat the army the AI built against it in nearly every pairing. The fix is in how the AI grows, what it builds and how it moves; unit stats and the crush rule are unchanged, and Difficulty still changes only reaction time, orders per decision and shard bonus (AI-10). Issues #174 to #177 and #181.

- **HAR-5**: The harness has a steamroll opponent, for measuring only: it is not a personality, Random never draws it and the menu does not offer it. It plays the build the game's owner beats the Hard AI with, at Hard's reaction time and orders per decision and with no shard bonus (a human player gets none). Opening: power as needed, a refinery at its second home field, a barracks training rifle squads until its first factory stands, then from the factory one crusher and then haulers. After that its barracks train anti-armour and rifle squads (about 55% anti-armour) and its factories crushers, and it grows to five barracks and five factories. It takes a third field, building toward it when it is out of placement reach, with four haulers at a field of thirty shard tiles or more and three at a smaller one. It builds no turret, lab, hero, artillery, scout unit or superweapon. It keeps its whole army at home, defending, until the army is worth 14 000 credits and holds at least six crushers; then it attacks once with everything, as one body with crush orders (AI-11, BAL-5), never retreats, sends later units after the army in groups, and masses again if the attack is wiped out. The same seeds and settings give the same game (HAR-1), and an AI that does not face it plays exactly as before. A match series plays the Hard AI of each personality against it: 8 seeds, Aegis against Vrax both ways round, from both sides, 96 games of up to 20 minutes. Baseline before the changes of this spec: the Hard AI won 19 of the 96 (as Raider 10 of 32, Balanced 5, Doomsday 4; as Aegis none of 48, as Vrax 19 of 48); the opponent's first attack set out at about minute 6 with an army worth about 19 500 credits, and its army peaked at about twice the value of the AI's.
- **AI-21**: Every AI keeps a standing scout, so it knows what the enemy is building. Once the building that trains its scout unit stands (Scout Buggy at the Vehicle Factory, Stalker at the Brood Pit), it trains one, ahead of other combat units from that building, and trains a replacement when it has none: no sooner than 30 s after a loss, or 60 s if the lost scout lived less than a minute, so it does not feed scouts to the same guns. The standing scout is never part of the army: it joins no wave, gathering or base defence, and the AI trains no second scout unit while it lives (Raider may, as raiders for its hit squads, AI-27). It watches rather than fights. It tours observation points round the enemy base, but not its front, where the enemy gathers and its waves set out; the points are 9 tiles out from the enemy buildings it knows (from the enemy start, read off the symmetric map, until it knows one). It goes to and fro along them, moving on when it reaches one, and a replacement starts on the other side. It keeps off ground within 8 tiles of enemy buildings and near armed enemy units it saw in the last 30 s, and moves along a way round that ground in hops of at most 4 tiles, so it decides again before it can drive into guns it has not seen; if no point can be reached that way it goes to its staging point. Whatever its health, when an armed enemy unit comes within 10 tiles of it (or within that unit's gun range plus 2 tiles, if that is farther) or a defence within 8, it flees 8 tiles straight away from the armed enemies it sees, and then tours the other way round. Until it has a scout unit the AI scouts as before with its fastest unit (AI-17), which then rejoins the army; every AI still finds an enemy building by minute 4 (AI-17), and Raider still scouts first (AI-14). The steamroll opponent keeps no standing scout (HAR-5). Difficulty changes none of this beyond reaction time and orders per decision (AI-10): a slower AI moves in hops and stops between them. Measured in headless matches on every map, from both sides, against the same matches with the old scouting (12 games each): at Hard the AI loses 1.0 scouts a game (was 2.7), its standing scout is alive at least 95% of the time, and it has seen 51% of the enemy army's value within the last minute (was 44%); at Medium it loses 1.1 (was 3.1), the scout is alive about 96% of the time, and it has seen 45% (was 40%). The match test requires, with the standing scout on: at Hard at most 2 scouts lost per AI per game, a standing scout alive at least 85% of the time and coverage of at least 40%; at Medium at most 2.5 lost, alive at least 80% and coverage of at least 36%. Amended for tank spam (#190): Every AI keeps two standing scouts (rules.twoScouts, on). The first tours the enemy base as before. The second watches the way between the bases: its post lies halfway along the line from its staging point to the enemy start and 6 tiles to the left of it (to the right when that is off the map), on open ground. It goes there in hops of at most 4 tiles along a way round ground closed to it (as the first does), waits there, and flees from threats exactly as the first does. A lost one is replaced as the first is (30 s, or 60 s after a young loss). Production trains a scout unit while it has fewer than two, one a decision. Counter-play (what it trains, SKM-6, AI-19) already uses only sightings from the last two minutes (sightings older than that are forgotten), and with none it keeps its default mix. Measured: against tank spam the second scout changed nothing (72 of 96 with it alone, 72 without); faction balance and the scout tests below still pass with it.
- **AI-22**: The AI turns its income into army as fast as a strong player does: production buildings come first. Measured against the steamroll opponent (HAR-5) before this change, the Hard AI earned more than the opponent with fewer haulers, but at the opponent's first attack it had four production buildings to the opponent's six, its second factory stood two minutes later (minute 4.5 against 2.5), and about 20 000 credits sat banked or paid into queues that had not built yet; its headquarters spent the time on its lab, turrets and extra power. Build order: after its first barracks, second refinery and first factory (AI-3) the AI builds a second factory, a second barracks and a third factory before its lab, any turret or its superweapon, with power only as its margin needs (AI-3). From then on, whenever every barracks and every factory it has is busy and it has more than 1 500 credits banked, its next building is another barracks or factory, whichever it has fewer of (a factory on a tie), up to five of each, ahead of everything but power. Its lab comes once it has five production buildings and that rule has nothing to build; turrets wait until it has six production buildings or its base has been attacked. Personalities keep their character: Doomsday builds its lab straight after its second factory (amending AI-15, which had it after the first) and so still orders it before Balanced does, and it grows by factories alone, another whenever every factory it has is busy and it has more than 1 500 banked, up to five, so its army stays tanks, artillery and its hero and barracks it leaves idle on purpose do not hold it back (AI-15); Raider builds no lab, turret or superweapon during its opening (AI-14). A building's queue is at most three units deep, five with more than 3 000 credits banked, and at most four artillery pieces are in production at once. Fields and haulers are unchanged (AI-18: three refineries and eight haulers at most). Difficulty changes none of this beyond reaction time, orders per decision and shard bonus (AI-10). The steamroll opponent keeps its own build (HAR-5). Tried and rejected, each measured over the 96-game steamroll series: taking further fields with more haulers (14 wins against 19 before: income already exceeded what the AI could spend, and each hauler takes a factory's time); the lab as soon as it has five production buildings (44 wins against 55 without) or seven (51); and queues only two deep (64 wins against 69 with deeper ones). Measured in Medium-against-Medium matches on every map: the second factory stands at minute 2.2 at the median (was about 4.5) and an AI has eight production buildings by minute 6. The match test requires the second factory by minute 3.5 and six production buildings by minute 6 at the median, and at most two harness violations a game (HAR-2).
- **AI-23**: The AI does not feed a losing fight. (a) A wave's strength counts its reinforcements: the retreat test (AI-8) compares the wave with the strongest it has been, not with what set out; before, a first wave of about 1 500 credits kept absorbing reinforcements until it had lost about 8 000. (b) Reinforcements join a wave only while the wave is at least as strong as the enemy it has seen within 11 tiles of it; otherwise they wait at the staging point. (c) A wave that is out comes home when an enemy force worth at least half the AI's whole army is seen within 20 tiles of its buildings: it attack-moves to a point on the enemy side of its base, within 5 tiles of its buildings and beside its turrets when it has any. (d) Stalemate: when no wave of its own has set out and its base has not been attacked for three minutes, and it has five barracks and five factories or an army worth at least 25 000 credits, it attacks if its army is at least as strong as the enemy army it has seen plus the defences at the target (parity instead of a clear advantage), so equal AIs do not wait for ever (ROS-4). (e) Raider keeps harassing from the start (AI-14), but a raid sets out only at a target where the defenders it has seen within 10 tiles in the last minute are worth less than half the raid; otherwise it picks the next economy target it knows, or waits. Retreats are otherwise as in AI-8. Difficulty changes none of this beyond reaction time, orders per decision and shard bonus (AI-10). Tried and rejected, each switched on alone over the 96-game steamroll series, against 55 wins without it: holding the army at the base while the enemy it has seen is worth at least 80% of its own (50 wins: the scout undercounts an army massed at home, and a held army did not stop the attack); retreating only when the wave can outrun what it sees (43 wins: a mixed wave never can, so every bad attack became a total loss); retreating as an attack-move (54); valuing the enemy army from its largest sighting or from the production buildings seen (53 and 54); and building crushers and artillery instead of squads against an army built round crushers (28 wins: that army wins equal-cost battles in the open, but in a match the barracks stood idle and the money went unspent).
- **AI-24**: The AI moves its infantry squads out of the way of crushers, reversing BAL-5's "AI infantry does not dodge crushers". When an enemy crusher it can see is within 3 tiles of one or more of its infantry squads, it gives those squads a Scatter order (CTL-18), no more than once every 2 s for the same squads; a squad given a Scatter order this way keeps its wave membership. The order comes out of the same share of the command budget as crush orders (BAL-5). Difficulty changes none of this beyond reaction time and orders per decision (AI-10). Measured: before, half to two thirds of the squads the AI lost ahead of the steamroll opponent's first attack were crushed; switched on alone this rule raised the Hard AI's wins in the steamroll series from 55 to 64 of 96.
- **AI-25**: The Hard AI holds its own against a steamroll. With a shard bonus of +30% it beat it more often than not: in the steamroll series (HAR-5, 96 games) it won at least two thirds, and each personality at least half of its 32 games. Measured with AI-21 to AI-24: 69 of 96 (Raider 22, Balanced 29, Doomsday 18; as Aegis 21 of 48, as Vrax all 48), from 19 of 96 before (10, 5 and 4; as Aegis none). Since AI-28 lowered Hard's shard bonus from +30% to +15%, because Hard was too hard, the Hard AI no longer meets that mark and wins about half: 51 of 96 (Raider 17, Balanced 22, Doomsday 12; as Aegis 10 of 48, as Vrax 41 of 48). The same series at other bonuses: 56 at +20% and 69 at +30%. With AI-26 and AI-27 it wins 47 (Raider 18, Balanced 21, Doomsday 8; as Aegis 11 of 48, as Vrax 36 of 48). The match test plays each personality's 32 games as its own CI job and fails if Raider wins fewer than 12, Balanced fewer than 16 or Doomsday fewer than 6 (floors under the measured figures, so a change that costs games still fails); the tally it prints gives the total.
- **AI-26**: Main waves vary their approach, so the AI's attacks do not always come the same way. When a main wave sets out it chooses one of three approaches to its target: straight, by the left flank or by the right flank. A flank's approach point lies halfway along the straight line from its staging point to the target and 10 tiles to that side of it, moved to the nearest open ground (ground the map does not block); a flank whose point would lie off the map or has no open ground within 6 tiles is not offered. It never chooses the approach its previous main wave chose, unless no other is offered. Of the others it takes the one farthest from where it last saw the enemy army: the middle, weighted by cost, of the armed enemy units it has seen in the last two minutes, leaving out those within 10 tiles of the target (the defenders stand where it is going whichever way it comes); the distance is measured to a flank's approach point and, for the straight approach, to the halfway point of the straight line. With no such sighting it takes the approaches in turn, starting from one drawn from its seed. The wave goes to its approach point and from there to the target, as one body all the way (AI-11): it advances in hops, closes up when strung out, and has passed the point once its centre is within 4 tiles of it; reinforcements that join it after that go by the point too. A wave that picks a new target on the way (its objective gone or reached) goes straight to it. Retreat, recall and the rule against feeding are unchanged (AI-8, AI-23). Raids choose their own way (AI-27), a Doomsday wave timed with a charged strike goes straight (AI-15), and the steamroll opponent always goes straight (HAR-5). Difficulty changes none of this beyond reaction time and orders per decision (AI-10), and the same seeds and settings give the same game (HAR-1). Measured over the 96-game steamroll series, this rule switched on alone against 51 wins without it: 52 with the flank 10 tiles out; tried and rejected, 14 tiles (44 wins: a slow army is too long on the way) and 6 tiles (47). With the rule on, of about 290 main waves about 45 went straight, 130 left and 115 right, and none took the same approach twice running; the match test requires that no AI repeats an approach and that each personality's series uses all three.
- **AI-27**: Hit squads go for the enemy economy from a side the army is not on. A hit squad is at least three units, moves as one body (AI-11) and is judged on its own: the retreat test (AI-8, AI-23 a) is applied to it alone, it gets no reinforcements unless stated here, a beaten one goes back to the staging point and its units rejoin the army, one that has done with its target moves on to the next economy target it knows (AI-14), and a threat to the base calls it home like any other group (AI-7). (a) Raider splits a raid in two when half of it can stand alone. When the raid that sets out has at least six units, each half is worth at least the armed enemy units it has seen in the last two minutes (with none seen there is no such limit), and it knows two economy targets (AI-14) at least 8 tiles apart that it may go for, each judged against the squad that would go there (AI-23 e), it sets out as two hit squads of about equal value, the units dealt out alternately from the dearest down, each to its own target: the nearest it may go for, and the one farthest from that. Each squad goes by the flank (AI-26's approach points) on its target's side of the line from the staging point to midway between the targets, straight if that flank is not offered, so the two arrive from different sides at about the same time. Reinforcements gathered at the staging point join the squad with fewer units left, under AI-11 and AI-23 b. A split raid is one attack in AI-14's and AI-16's counts, and counts as beaten back once, when both squads have ended and either was beaten. Otherwise the raid sets out as one body, as before. So that it has the units for this, Raider may train scout units beyond its standing scout while it plays as Raider (amending AI-21), at most three, and those belong to the army. (b) Balanced and Doomsday send one hit squad beside their main wave. When a main wave sets out and the units setting out include at least three fast units (a top speed of at least 45, which the Skitterer, Scout Buggy and Stalker have; never the standing scout, AI-21, or a hero), the fastest of them, at most a tenth of the army's value and at least three, set out as a hit squad at the economy target it may go for (AI-23 e, judged against the squad) that is farthest from the main wave's target, by the flank on the other side from the main wave's approach (when the wave goes straight, the flank on the target's side). They are not part of the wave, and the wave's strength and its retreat test do not count them. The wave keeps at least five units, so a hit squad never leaves a lone wave behind (HAR-2), and only one hit squad is out at a time. With no such target, or too few fast units, no hit squad sets out and those units go with the wave. In practice only a Vrax army sends one: Aegis has no fast unit but the Scout Buggy, which Balanced and Doomsday train only as their standing scout. A hit squad is not a wave in the harness's counts (waves, first wave, suicidal or lone waves) and starts no wave cooldown. Difficulty changes none of this beyond reaction time and orders per decision (AI-10); the steamroll opponent sends no hit squad (HAR-5). Measured over the 96-game steamroll series, each part switched on alone against 51 wins without it: the split raid 53 (against an opponent that masses its army at home only 2 raids split); the hit squad 50 (134 sent). Tried and rejected: splitting whatever army the enemy has (40 wins, Raider 6 of its 32 against 17: half a raid meets the whole enemy army), splitting when each half is worth half that army (48, Raider 14), and a hit squad of a fifth of the army (45). With AI-26 and both parts on, the Hard AI wins 47 of the 96 against 51 without them, and 40 against 45 on eight further seeds: the variety costs a few games, most of them Doomsday's (17 of 64 against 25).

## Tank spam (issue #190)

The owner still beat the Hard AI as Vrax by massing Bulwark Tanks and kiting: Bulwarks fire on the move (BAL-11) at range 6 and are faster than the Brute (42 against 38), which fights at range 1.9 and so cannot catch them. The Glare Skimmer (SKM-1 to SKM-6) is Vrax's answer in units and its AI use (SKM-6) replaces the issue's Spitter-heavy mix; the requirements below measure tank spam and change how the AI plays against it. Difficulty still changes only reaction time, orders per decision and shard bonus (AI-10). Every number below is from the Balance workflow (HAR-6).

- **HAR-7**: The harness has a tank-spam opponent, for measuring only: the steamroll opponent (HAR-5) with the named variant `tankspam` (STEAMROLL_VARIANT=tankspam, which later parts may change, such as `tankspam,menders:1`). It plays Aegis. After its opening (rifle squads from its one barracks until its first factory stands, HAR-5) its barracks train nothing but one anti-armour squad for each Field Mender it has, and its five factories train Bulwark Tanks and haulers. It mines its two home fields only and keeps its unit queues two deep, so its credits go into factories before queues; it launches its attack with an army worth 14 000 credits and at least 12 crushers. Its tanks kite: a tank that sees a ground enemy whose gun reaches less far than its own and that it cannot run over (not an infantry squad, which it crushes, BAL-5), within that enemy's reach plus 3 tiles, moves 3 tiles straight away from such enemies (the nearest counting most; turning up to 90 degrees round rock or water), firing as it goes (BAL-11); at most 6 such orders a decision, nearest threats first, out of its budget of orders. It does not kite from what outranges it (the Skimmer). It plays without Field Menders, or with them in its ball (`menders:1`, AI-29's count and loading; its tanks do not fall back). A match series plays the Hard AI as Vrax of each personality against both flavours: 8 seeds, both seats, 16 games a personality and flavour, 96 in all. A first draft that took a third field by crawling toward it and kept deep queues (as HAR-5) lost almost every game: the Hard AI won 89 of 96, because the opponent's credits sat in queues of three factories and its HQ stalled on the crawl. With the home fields and shallow queues it reaches five factories by minute 5 and an army of about 25 000 credits by minute 6. Eight factories instead of five made no difference (74 of 96 against 72).
- **AI-30**: The AI does not chase what it cannot catch (rules.noChase, on). While the armed enemy units it has seen in the last two minutes are mostly heavy armour by cost (SKM-6), a melee crusher of its own (weapon range under 3 tiles, not a hero: the Brute) that, at every decision for 3 seconds, sees heavy enemy armour faster than itself within 10 tiles moving away from it (farther from it than at its previous decision) with no enemy within its reach plus one tile, stops chasing: for 5 seconds it leaves the army's orders and holds where it stands when ranged units of its own are within 8 tiles of it, else goes to its nearest defence within 15 tiles (beside it, on the side toward its headquarters), else to the middle of its ranged army, else to its staging point. Measured against tank spam (96 games, all other new rules off), against 72 wins without it: falling back to a defence or the staging point at once 49 (rejected: the Brutes left the fight and the Skimmers stood alone); holding beside the ranged army 84. That version cost Vrax in faction balance (Aegis 30, Vrax 5, 13 undecided of 48, against 21-14-13 without it) and in the steamroll series (41 of 96 against 45), where the Aegis AI's tanks that turn away are not kiting; requiring the chase to have gone on for 3 seconds keeps the gain against tank spam and gives that back: with the second scout (AI-21) and the rest off, the Hard AI as Vrax won 78 of 96 against tank spam, 44 of 96 in the steamroll series (45 with none of this spec's rules; as Vrax 42 of 48 both ways), and faction balance stood at Aegis 25, Vrax 11, 12 undecided of 48 (24-14-10 without the second scout).
- **AI-31**: Tried and kept switched off (rules.ballRaid, off): while the enemy is mostly heavy armour and the middle, by cost, of the heavy armour it has seen in the last 30 seconds lies at least 20 tiles from every enemy building it knows, Balanced and Doomsday send their fastest units (at most a quarter of the army's value, at least three) as a hit squad (AI-27) at the economy target farthest from that armour, by the flank away from it, at most once in 30 seconds; a threat to the base does not call it home. Measured against tank spam: 70 of 96 against 72 without; in faction balance it favoured Aegis (33-10-5 against 21-14-13).
- **AI-33**: Tried and kept switched off (rules.screen, off): against mostly heavy armour, Vrax's Brood Pits keep one Brute for every three Skimmers, alive and queued, to screen them (the rest of the issue's Spitter-heavy mix, beside SKM-6). Measured against tank spam: 71 of 96 against 72 without; faction balance unchanged.
- **AI-32**: The Hard AI as Vrax holds its own against tank spam. Measured in the tank-spam series (HAR-7, 96 games, wins of the Hard AI as Vrax, by personality Raider / Balanced / Doomsday out of 16 each): with none of the rules of this spec, without Menders 6 / 11 / 12 and with Menders 12 / 15 / 16, 72 in all; with AI-30 alone 40 / 44 by flavour (84); with AI-21's second scout alone 72; AI-31 alone 70; AI-33 alone 71; as the game plays (AI-30 with its 3-second patience and the second scout), without Menders 7 / 14 / 14 and with Menders 13 / 15 / 15, 78 in all. So Hard's shard bonus stays at +15% (AI-28): behaviour alone is well over the owner's mark of about half, and the AI-28 ladder and HAR-3 are untouched. Against this opponent Raider without Menders is the weak case: its fast, cheap army meets massed tanks head on. With Menders in its ball the opponent did worse, not better (each Mender is 750 credits not spent on a tank). The opponent is still a bot: it does not reproduce how the owner beats the Hard AI (the owner then plays it, issue #190). The match test plays each personality and flavour as its own CI job and fails if, of its 16 games, Raider without Menders wins fewer than 4, or any other fewer than 8 (half).
