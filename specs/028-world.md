# 028 World detail (milestone 28)

A deeper 3D world in the same art direction (warm late-afternoon sun, cool shadows): surface detail on every model, richer terrain, a horizon past the map edge, GPU smoke, fire and dust, debris, a camera that tilts with zoom, and new harvester models and motion. Everything is generated in code; no texture or model file is added. Nothing here changes the simulation. Issue #145.

- **R3D-23**: Every unit and building model shows procedural surface detail at play zoom: edge wear and darker shading in creases. No regular grid, seam pattern or chequered pattern is visible on any unit or building. The Bulwark Tank, Scout Buggy and Hammer Artillery each wear a distinct base colour (the tank green, the artillery pale, the buggy warm) in clean, solid armour panels with markings and lit accents, with no camouflage blotches, so they can be told apart at a distance. It is generated in code, with no texture files, and adds no draw calls (the instanced batches of R3D-13 are unchanged).
- **R3D-24**: Terrain shows more detail. Cliff faces show rock layers, and the ground breaks up into patches of grass, dirt, gravel and rubble, with fine surface detail. All of it comes from tile coordinates only, so it is the same every visit and independent of the simulation's random numbers.
- **R3D-25**: Beyond the map edge the world continues to a horizon: distant land fading into haze under a sky gradient that matches the light. At any zoom or tilt the view never shows empty space.
- **R3D-26**: Smoke, fire and dust are drawn by a GPU particle system with a fixed budget: explosions, burning buildings and wrecks, and dust behind vehicles on dry ground. Particles are animated on the GPU, not moved one by one on the CPU. Low uses a smaller budget, or none.
- **R3D-27**: A destroyed vehicle or building throws a few chunks of debris that arc, bounce and settle, then fade within a few seconds. Debris is drawn by the renderer only and limited in number.
- **R3D-28**: The camera tilts with zoom. At the default zoom it keeps today's pitch; zooming in lowers it smoothly to a more cinematic angle, and zooming out returns it. The heading never changes. Selection, orders, cursors, the minimap and pick reach (CTL-1) keep working through the projection seam at every pitch. The fly-in (CAM-4) still ends exactly on the player's camera.
- **R3D-29**: With everything in this milestone on, High holds 60 fps on Intel integrated graphics in ordinary play, measured with the frame-time readout in the showcase scenario. The heaviest case, a 200-unit brawl, runs at about 54 fps on that graphics chip at 1920 by 1080, at full resolution (it stays above the 50 fps at which PERF-2 would lower it); the owner accepted this on 2026-11-14 (#150), after the cuts that keep the picture (simpler shadow shapes PERF-4, the lighter post chain and fewer effect lights PERF-5) and a cheaper ground shader that was tried and gained under 1 fps.
- **R3D-30**: The Shard Hauler is a heavy, sealed mining machine, an original design. Six large wheels in armoured hubs carry a long sealed chassis. At the front is a wedge-shaped cab under a tinted canopy, matching the Scout Buggy; behind it a smooth-sided hopper with a lit rim, in which the load glows shard-coloured as it fills. Across the front is the mining head: an enclosed drum housing on two sealed arms, with no exposed hydraulic rams. While mining, the head lowers to the ground and the drum chews into the field, throwing chips and dust, while crystals are seen travelling up a covered conveyor through an open window strip into the hopper. While driving, the head is raised clear of the ground. To unload, it backs onto the dock pad, the rear hatch swings clear and the whole hopper tips up at the front to about 45° about a hinge at its rear, so the load visibly slides out into the refinery's receiving trough; the hopper lowers again before it drives off. It has weight: when it comes to a stop the nose dips once and the suspension settles, damped, without bouncing on; the head bobs as it chews; and a cooling vent breathes a short burst of pale vapour on throttle (it has no exhaust stack).
- **R3D-31**: The Gorger feeds without tipping forward. Its body stays level, with only a slow side-to-side sway. Its two feeding claws are its front pair of limbs, legs evolved for gathering: each is rooted at the side of the body just behind the head, jointed like its legs, and works on its own side only, so the claws never cross each other or reach across the front of the maw. Their outer segments end in flat, faceted, blade-like rakes in a shard-like finish: darker and more lustrous than the chitin, almost metallic, with faint crystal-teal edges, blending into the chitin at the joints. While feeding, each claw starts out to the side and sweeps in a scooping stroke of about a quarter turn (roughly 90 degrees) across the ground toward the mouth, low and closing on the inward stroke, then lifts a little and swings back out; the two claws scoop together and meet beside the mouth without crossing each other, each staying on its own side of the centre plane, with only a modest rise and fall, never a flap, and the mouth takes the shards in; the throat pulses as it swallows, and the belly swells with the load. To unload, the belly contracts in waves and it disgorges the glowing mass at the intake, still level. The menu scene (MENU-10) and the unit sheet preview (INF-6) show the same models, materials, surface detail and motion as the match, and the menu's ground has the match's ground detail. The menu's working hauler throws chips and grit from its cutter or maw, and puffs exhaust, as GPU particles on High only (never on Low or with reduced motion).
- **R3D-32**: The Hammer Artillery is a rail-gun artillery piece, an original design. A long, low tracked hull with six or seven road wheels under armoured skirts carries a large, enclosed, angular turret set toward the rear. A long rail-gun barrel, two parallel rails in a braced frame with cyan-lit coil rings along it, reaches out over the front of the hull and rests in a travel lock on the glacis while the gun is idle. Hull and turret are sealed, faceted shells in pale panels; the turret carries a sensor array, a short antenna and a capacitor housing at its rear; it has no open deck, no outriggers and no visible crew. When it fires, the turret turns and the barrel elevates steeply, recoils and returns, and every shell leaves along the barrel (AIM-1). A shell is never seen leaving while the barrel is still rising: the shell, flash and recoil show only once the barrel has finished elevating.
- **R3D-33**: The Scout Buggy is a fast, light, high-tech scout car, an original design: a low, wedge-shaped armoured body on four long-travel wheels, with a closed cab under a tinted canopy and a sensor mast at the back. No driver or crew figure is drawn. Its wheels steer and its suspension soaks up the ground as it races.
- **R3D-34**: Buildings carry fine detail, since few are on screen at once. Every building of both factions shows small-scale features beyond its main masses, placed where they make sense for what the building does. Aegis buildings: panel seams and armour plates, vents and grilles, pipes and cable runs, railings and ladders, antennae and sensor masts, hazard markings, doors and hatches, small lit windows and status lights. Vrax buildings: ribs and plates of chitin, veins and tendrils, pores and vents, spines, membranes, root-like footings, and dim bioluminescent spots. The detail is shape and surface, not motion: nothing new moves, blinks or pulses, and glows stay at their present brightness. A building keeps its silhouette, footprint, height, team-coloured parts, muzzles, dock and damage looks, so picking, placement, build icons and shots are unchanged. Cost: no building is drawn with more than twice its previous triangles or more than 6,000 in all, shadows still come from the stand-in (PERF-4), and the showcase and the 200-unit brawl run no slower on High than before.
