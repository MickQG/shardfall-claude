# 028 World detail (milestone 28)

A deeper 3D world in the same art direction (warm late-afternoon sun, cool shadows): surface detail on every model, richer terrain, a horizon past the map edge, GPU smoke, fire and dust, debris, a camera that tilts with zoom, and new harvester models and motion. Everything is generated in code; no texture or model file is added. Nothing here changes the simulation. Issue #145.

- **R3D-23**: Every unit and building model shows procedural surface detail at play zoom: panel lines, edge wear and darker shading in creases. It is generated in code, with no texture files, and adds no draw calls (the instanced batches of R3D-13 are unchanged).
- **R3D-24**: Terrain shows more detail. Cliff faces show rock layers, and the ground breaks up into patches of grass, dirt, gravel and rubble, with fine surface detail. All of it comes from tile coordinates only, so it is the same every visit and independent of the simulation's random numbers.
- **R3D-25**: Beyond the map edge the world continues to a horizon: distant land fading into haze under a sky gradient that matches the light. At any zoom or tilt the view never shows empty space.
- **R3D-26**: Smoke, fire and dust are drawn by a GPU particle system with a fixed budget: explosions, burning buildings and wrecks, and dust behind vehicles on dry ground. Particles are animated on the GPU, not moved one by one on the CPU. Low uses a smaller budget, or none.
- **R3D-27**: A destroyed vehicle or building throws a few chunks of debris that arc, bounce and settle, then fade within a few seconds. Debris is drawn by the renderer only and limited in number.
- **R3D-28**: The camera tilts with zoom. At the default zoom it keeps today's pitch; zooming in lowers it smoothly to a more cinematic angle, and zooming out returns it. The heading never changes. Selection, orders, cursors, the minimap and pick reach (CTL-1) keep working through the projection seam at every pitch. The fly-in (CAM-4) still ends exactly on the player's camera.
- **R3D-29**: With everything in this milestone on, High holds 60 fps with 200 units on Intel integrated graphics, measured with the frame-time readout in the showcase scenario and in a 200-unit brawl.
- **R3D-30**: The Shard Hauler is a heavy, armoured industrial harvester, an original design. A long chassis on tracks or six heavy wheels carries a raised armoured cab at the front and a large boxed hopper behind it. Across the front is a broad harvesting head: a housed, toothed intake drum on two hydraulic arms. While mining, the head lowers to the ground and the drum chews into the field, throwing chips and dust, while a conveyor or chute visibly carries crystals up into the hopper, which fills with the load. While driving, the head is raised clear of the ground. To unload, it backs onto the dock pad and the hopper discharges through a rear hatch or tipping floor into the refinery's intake. It has weight: the suspension settles as it stops, the head bobs as it chews, and exhaust puffs on throttle.
- **R3D-31**: The Gorger feeds without tipping forward. Its body stays level, with only a slow side-to-side sway. While feeding, mandibles and feeding tendrils reach out, grip crystals and draw them into the maw; the throat pulses as it swallows, and the belly swells with the load. To unload, the belly contracts in waves and it disgorges the glowing mass at the intake, still level. The menu scene (MENU-10) and the unit sheet preview (INF-6) use the same harvester models and motion as the match.
