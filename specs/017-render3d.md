# 017 Real-time 3D renderer slice (milestone 17, ADR 0005)

The world drawn in real-time 3D with three.js behind a switch, alongside the classic view until the owner approves it. Gameplay, HUD, minimap and input rules are unchanged.

- **R3D-1**: `?renderer=3d` draws the world with the 3D renderer for the whole session: chosen once at startup, never part of the simulation; without it the classic view is used unchanged.
- **R3D-2**: The 3D terrain is built from the map grid alone: one ground surface per tile at its level's height, rock cliff walls down to every lower neighbour (the sunward walls warmer), water surfaces on blocked low tiles, and ground colours that come only from tile coordinates.
- **R3D-3**: Every unit and building has a procedural 3D model shaped from bevelled plates, cylinders, domes and lathed shells (not voxel blocks) that keeps its kind's silhouette and role: team colour on its marked parts, glowing parts drawn unlit and bright, crystals as faceted clusters that shrink as they deplete, props as rocks and flora. Infantry squads show one figure per standing member; turrets turn on their own pivot; every weapon has named muzzle points.
- **R3D-4**: The 3D camera follows the same camera state as the classic view (screen-centre point and zoom): it looks along the isometric heading at a fixed pitch from a distance set by the zoom, so pan, zoom, centring and the minimap behave the same.
- **R3D-5**: One projector per renderer maps tiles to screen pixels and screen pixels to ground tiles; selection, orders, hit-testing and test hooks use it, so clicking a unit in the 3D view selects it and right-clicking the ground orders a move there.
- **R3D-6**: The 3D view lights the scene with a warm sun that casts shadows (off on the low preset) and cool ambient light, and shows combat as glowing tracers, pooled point lights, fireballs and smoke.
- **R3D-7**: The showcase in the 3D view runs on both quality presets without page errors under the software renderer used in CI, and shows a frame-time readout.
- **R3D-8**: Shot events name the shooter (`src`, a unit or building id) and death events name what died (`id`, `kind`). Events never enter the state hash, so the simulation stays deterministic.
- **R3D-9**: Shots leave the barrel: tracers, muzzle flashes and projectiles start at the shooter's muzzle point in world space (turret aim included, multi-barrel weapons alternate, each standing squad member fires from its own weapon), and the barrel recoils. Turrets traverse fast (a quarter turn in under a quarter of a second) and track their target, and a shot leaves a turret only once it points at the target, or after at most 0.3 s; the shell's drawn flight is shortened by that wait so it still lands with the damage. Projectiles with a flight time travel for exactly that time (shells and acid arc, rockets leave smoke), and every hit plays an impact effect on arrival. Melee units lunge at their target.
- **R3D-10**: Movement is animated in the renderer only: each unit's drawn position follows its simulated position through a short critically damped spring (under a tenth of a second behind), so snapshots that arrive unevenly never show as stalls, jumps or jitter, and jumps of several tiles are taken at once; hulls keep their heading when they barely move and otherwise turn quickly (vehicles at least 5 radians a second, so a change of direction reads as a pivot, not a slide), the tracks or wheels on each side roll by their own distance (the inside track runs backwards when a tank pivots), and turn toward their travel direction at a bounded rate, wheels and road wheels spin with the distance travelled, wheeled and tracked vehicles pitch and roll with acceleration and turning, infantry and alien walkers play a gait whose stride follows the distance travelled and stand still when stopped, aircraft hover, bob and bank into turns with spinning rotors or beating wings, and every unit sits on the ground height under it.
- **R3D-11**: Terrain has detail beyond flat tiles, all from tile coordinates: cliff walls are faceted rock with strata, watertight with the ground and identical for the same map; the ground has fine relief that catches the low sun; water ripples and glints; rocks, grass and alien flora are scattered away from buildings and shard fields.
- **R3D-12**: The high preset adds bloom on glowing parts, fire and tracers, anti-aliasing, dust behind moving vehicles, smoke and fire on damaged buildings (below the GFX-2 thresholds), and deaths that leave something behind: vehicles become burning wrecks that sink away, infantry and aliens fall, aircraft crash, buildings collapse in fire. Vrax weapons have their own bioluminescent flair rather than fire and smoke: a glowing green spit burst with droplets at the mouth or stinger, acid globs that glow, drip and trail vapour in flight, stings and spines that leave a short glowing wake and burst in a green puff, claw strikes as three raking glowing arcs with spattered ichor, and acid that splashes in a crown, keeps bubbling and leaves a glowing puddle that fades within a few seconds. Tracers and muzzle flashes are subtle: small-arms tracers are thin and dim and their flashes small, and no shot glows brighter than an explosion. Scorch marks are soft, uneven burnt blobs on the ground under the blast (plateaus included), never a visible square. The low preset drops bloom, dust and shadows and shortens wrecks.
- **R3D-13**: Units, buildings, crystals and props are drawn as instanced batches per model part and team, culled to the view, so draw calls do not grow with the number of units; models, shader programs and vertex buffers are prepared while loading, so first sight of an army or the first explosion never stalls; the frame-time readout also shows draw calls, triangles and CPU time, so the budget of Constitution rule 8 (about 200 units, integrated graphics) can be checked on the high preset.
- **R3D-14**: Cliff tops, shorelines and shard ground follow organic contours instead of tile edges. Every point near a contour (a tile edge between different levels, or between water and land) is moved by a smooth warp that depends on its position alone: edges wave, and corners where one tile of four differs round off toward it. Corners round over a wide arc, up to two tiles across. The warp never moves a point more than 0.3 tiles, is zero two tiles or more from any contour and across the map border, and never folds or squeezes the ground; cliff walls, tile tops, water, the painted ground, scatter, props and crystals all follow it, and units stand on the drawn ground. Shard ground is an uneven patch blended across the shard tiles, and crystal clusters sit off-centre in their tiles. Shores come from a wetness field blended over a tile and a half and broken up by noise: the water surface fades in across a wavy, rounded shore line with lighter, foamy edges, over a beach of sand and wet sand between the grass and the lake bed; it is opaque well inside the water and never covers walkable land beyond a sliver (an eighth of a tile). Reeds grow at the waterline and in the shallows, pebbles lie on the beaches and boulders sit at the foot of cliffs. Gameplay stays on the tile grid.
- **R3D-15**: Squad members move on their own, in the renderer only, around their squad's position and within about half a tile of it: marching, they keep a loose formation that shifts, each with its own stride, running to catch up; idle, they stroll to new spots and look around, each on its own timer; firing, they form a ragged line facing the target, some kneeling and holding their spot while the others sidestep between bursts. They never pick a spot on other ground (a cliff edge or water). A member who dies falls where it stood and sinks away while the rest re-form. The randomness is a hash of the unit, so the same squad behaves the same every run; selection, health bars and the simulation still treat the squad as one unit.
