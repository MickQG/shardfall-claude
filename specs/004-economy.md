# 004 Economy, production and tech (milestone 4)

Shards are the single resource. Haulers gather shards and deliver them to refineries; credits pay for structures and units, which are produced from queues, limited by power and a three-tier tech tree.

- **ECO-1**: Players start with the credits and buildings given in the game config; a player snapshot exposes the viewer's credits and power balance.
- **ECO-2**: A hauler gathers shards from a field into its load, drives to the nearest own refinery, unloads, and the delivered amount is added to credits while the field shrinks by the same amount.
- **ECO-3**: A hauler repeats this loop on its own; with no refinery it waits instead of wandering and resumes once one exists; a stop order halts the loop until a harvest order.
- **ECO-4**: Shard tiles deplete when emptied and seed tiles regrow slowly.
- **ECO-5**: Power output and drain are summed from completed buildings; while drain exceeds output, production progresses at half speed.
- **PRD-1**: Queueing an item needs its prerequisites and enough credits, deducts the cost, and cancelling refunds it in full.
- **PRD-2**: An item completes after its build time in ticks at full power, and after twice as long at low power.
- **PRD-3**: Structure placement must be within build range of an own building, on explored, unblocked, unoccupied tiles; an invalid placement is rejected and the ready item is kept.
- **PRD-4**: A placed structure blocks pathing, and units standing on its footprint are moved off it.
- **PRD-5**: A produced unit appears beside its producing building and walks to the rally point; only buildings that can train the unit kind may be the source.
- **PRD-6**: Items whose prerequisite buildings are not owned (the tech tree) cannot be queued.
- **PRD-7**: Selling a building refunds half its cost and frees its tiles; repairing restores hit points at a credit cost.
- **PRD-8**: Queues are first-in-first-out with a fixed maximum length of 20 items per building.
- **PRD-9**: Ctrl-clicking an item in the build or train list queues five of it at once; each is queued only while there is room in the queue and enough credits.
- **PRD-10**: Right-click cancels a queued item even when the list is full or the item cannot be afforded; Ctrl + right-click cancels up to five of that kind at once, newest first.
- **FOG-5**: Enemy buildings appear in a player snapshot only on visible tiles, and the last-seen ones are remembered as ghosts until their tile is seen again.
- **DET-1**: An economy scenario with the same seed and commands yields the same state hash; changing a command changes it.
- **UI-1**: The shared placement check agrees with the simulation, so the UI ghost is green exactly when a placement would be accepted.
- **MAP-2**: The skirmish map is point-symmetric; both start positions are connected, and every shard field is reachable from both starts.
- **MAP-3**: The skirmish map has water (blocked ground at level 0), mirrored like the rest of the map: a pond on each base's outer flank, clear of the base and its exits, and lakes beside the mid-map routes that narrow them without closing them. Water never covers a start, a starting building or a shard field.
- **MAP-4**: There are three skirmish maps, chosen in the menu (the choice is remembered) or with `?map=`: Crossroads (the default, MAP-2 and MAP-3), Riverbed and Highlands; an unknown map falls back to Crossroads. Every map is 96 by 96 tiles and point-symmetric, raised ground is impassable rock, both starts are connected, every shard field is reachable from both starts and lies on open ground, the starting forces stand on open ground, without overlapping, with no water within 8 tiles of a start and at least 20 shard tiles within 14 tiles of each HQ, both bases are equally far by road from each other and from every shard field, the 3D terrain of every map waves and rounds off without folding, and the AI plays every map to a finish (the CI harness: Hard beats Easy on each map).
- **MAP-5**: Riverbed: the bases sit at the middle of the north-east and south-west edges of the map (in the default isometric view), either side of a river that winds from the west corner to the east corner and is crossed in only three places: a land bridge at the centre, over the richest field, and a ford toward each flank; each base has two home fields either side of it, and rich fields lie on both banks. Both bases are equally far by road from each other and from every shard field.
- **MAP-6**: Highlands: the bases sit at the east and west corners of the map (in the default isometric view), facing each other. A ring of ridges, stepping down one level at a time, walls in a central basin with a pool at its heart and the richest fields beside it; the basin is entered only by a pass in the middle of each side and a wide gap at each of the two corners facing the bases (so the straight road between the bases runs through it), and mesas guard the approaches from each base. Raised ground is rock on every map, so ridges are walls rather than high ground to stand on.
- **ECO-6**: Training a harvester costs less than a refinery, so a refinery's free first harvester is never the cheapest way to add harvesters.
- **ECO-7**: Every refinery has exactly one dock pad (#113): a tile just outside its footprint, beside one of its sides, chosen when the refinery is placed as the spot nearest the nearest shards, with ties broken by rules a half turn of the map leaves unchanged, so mirrored refineries always get mirrored pads and every map stays the same for both players (MAP-2, MAP-4). The snapshot shows the pad, the 3D view draws the refinery's dock on it (the Aegis hazard pad and hopper, the Vrax feeding maw), and no building may be placed over it. A hauler unloads only on the pad: it drives onto it, unloads (unchanged: 10 shards a tick), then frees the pad and drives straight back to its field. The AI places refineries with the pad toward the shards and keeps the ground around a pad clear.
- **ECO-8**: While a refinery's pad is taken, a hauler arriving within a few tiles of it joins a line and waits on a tile beside the pad; the line is first come, first served, and the next hauler takes the pad as soon as it is free. A hauler choosing where to deliver counts each hauler already at a refinery's pad as a few tiles further away, so haulers spread over several refineries. With three haulers on one refinery the income stays within 10% of what it was before the pad when the field lies beside the refinery, and within 15% with the field off any side; both starting sides earn within 3% of each other.
- **ECO-9**: No hauler waits forever: a unit parked on the pad is pushed aside as the hauler drives on; if the pad stays out of reach the hauler unloads beside the refinery instead; if the refinery dies, haulers on its pad or in its line go to another refinery (or wait for one, ECO-3); a hauler that has waited in line a long time moves to a less busy refinery if one would serve it sooner; and a stopped or redirected hauler gives up its place.
