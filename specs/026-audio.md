# 026 Audio (milestone 26)

The game sounds like a big-budget RTS while every sound stays synthesized in code: no audio files and no voice acting. Combat sounds are layered, each unit kind answers with its own non-voice sound, the mix holds up in a large battle, and the music swells with the fighting. Issue #143.

- **AUD-5**: Every weapon kind, explosion size and death has a sound built from at least two layers: a sharp attack and a body or tail. Each play varies slightly in pitch and timing, so the same sound never plays identically twice in a row.
- **AUD-6**: Each faction has its own timbre. Aegis weapons, machines and buildings sound mechanical and metallic (inharmonic, ringing partials). Vrax sound organic and wet (filtered noise with formant sweeps, low clicks and gurgles).
- **AUD-7**: Acknowledgement sounds: selecting your own units, or giving them a command, plays a short non-voice sound particular to that unit kind. Selecting and commanding sound different. The kind used is the one with the most selected units. It plays at most once per selection or command, at most once every 150 ms, and only for the player who acts. It replaces the generic order sounds of UI-14.
- **AUD-8**: Moving units near the middle of the screen make movement sounds by class: tracks and engines for vehicles, heavy footfalls for walkers and heroes, steps for infantry, scuttling for Vrax swarms. Movement sounds are low and soft: rumbles, thumps and patters, never hiss. Each class plays as one loop whose level follows how many units of that class are moving on screen, and it is silent when none are.
- **AUD-9**: Every world sound pans left or right by where it happens on screen and gets quieter with distance from the middle of the screen. Sounds outside the viewer's interest are still not played (AUD-1).
- **AUD-10**: Each map has a generated ambience: lapping water while water is on screen; a distant rumble while the player's forces fight off-screen. No continuous sound carries hiss or static.
- **AUD-11**: UI sounds: hovering and clicking menu and HUD buttons, a construction finishing, a unit becoming ready, and a refused action. They play through the same engine and obey Mute all. Hovering plays only when the pointer moves onto a button that can be used now (in the HUD, an item that can be bought, trained or placed). Moving off a button, across its background or between its parts is silent. The click plays only on such a button. A refused click keeps its own refusal sound.
- **AUD-12**: The mix holds up in a large battle. At most 24 sounds play at once, and the quietest or farthest are dropped first. A limiter on the master output keeps a 200-unit battle from clipping.
- **AUD-13**: The music has a calm layer and a battle layer that crossfade. The battle layer's strength follows how many of the player's units and buildings dealt or took damage in the last 5 seconds, anywhere on the map: one fight gives a light pulse, and many give the full layer. It fades out about 10 s after the fighting stops. Both layers stay in key, and the notes remain a pure function of the seed (AUD-2).
- **AUD-14**: The menu score and the in-match music carry a short theme for each faction: martial for Aegis, eerie and pulsing for Vrax. In the menu the picked faction's theme plays; in a match, the player's. No voice in the music is bell-like, chime-like or brass-like.
- **AUD-15**: A superweapon launch and a superweapon strike each play a musical stinger over the score, in key.
- **AUD-16**: Music off (MENU-15) silences all music but not sound effects, and Music volume scales only the music. Mute all silences everything. The default music level sits clearly audible under the effects (about 5 dB above the original score).
