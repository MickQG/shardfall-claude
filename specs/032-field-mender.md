# 032 Field Mender (milestone 32)

Issue #192: a new Aegis unit, the Field Mender, a repair vehicle that carries one infantry squad. Original design and name. The stats are a first draft, tuned in CI.

- **CMB-15**: The Field Mender is an Aegis vehicle trained at the Motor Pool (tier 2, needing nothing else): heavy armour, 350 hit points, speed 40, cost 750. It has no weapon of its own and never crushes anything.
- **CMB-16**: A Field Mender repairs one damaged friendly vehicle at a time within 3 tiles (its centre to the vehicle's edge), at 6 hit points a second (the first draft's rate; lowered to 4 after the faction-balance matches, then raised back to 6 at the owner's request after playing it, issue #196): the one with the smallest share of its maximum health left, the most hit points missing breaking a tie. Vehicles are every ground unit that is not an infantry squad, heroes included; it never repairs infantry, buildings, enemies or itself this way. It chooses again several times a second, so a vehicle hurt worse that comes into reach is taken next, and it stops at full health. The snapshot reports which unit it is mending.
- **CMB-17**: A Field Mender repairs itself at 3 hit points a second at all times, in or out of combat, also while it repairs another vehicle, up to its maximum health.
- **SQD-3**: A Field Mender carries one squad: a Rifleman or Rocketeer squad, never a hero. A board order sends the player's listed squads of those kinds to the carrier; they neither fire nor turn aside on the way, and the first to reach it climbs in. A carrier that is full, gone or the enemy's takes nobody: the squads then stop where they are. Any other order to a squad on its way cancels the boarding. The snapshot reports the passenger's id, kind, health and rank on its carrier.
- **SQD-4**: A passenger fires its own weapon from inside, at its own range and rate, measured from the carrier, also while the carrier moves; it needs a clear line of fire (CMB-14) and only targets enemies its owner can see. Its damage scales with its figures and rank as outside (SQD-1, VET-1), and it earns experience for what it deals.
- **SQD-5**: A squad inside cannot be targeted, hit by shots or splash, struck by a superweapon or crushed. If the carrier is destroyed, by fire or by a hero running it over, the squad dies with it, and both count as lost.
- **SQD-6**: An unload order lets the squad out beside the carrier, on an open tile next to it, with the same id, health, rank and experience it had, idle and with no order.
- **SQD-7**: The Field Mender counts as a combat unit wherever the controls ask for one (issue #196): a box holding tanks or infantry keeps it (CTL-2), `Q` selects it (CTL-6), an attack-move reaches it (CTL-13), and with it selected an enemy under the pointer shows the attack cursor and a right-click there orders an attack (CUR-1, CUR-5). Harvesters stay the only units that are not combat units.
- **SQD-8**: An attack order to a Field Mender carrying a squad sends it toward the target until the target is in its passenger's range with a clear line of fire (CMB-14), and it holds there, following the target if it moves; its passenger fires at that target ahead of any other while it can (SQD-4). An empty Mender follows the target to within its repair range (3 tiles) and holds there. On an attack-move a Mender carrying a squad stops for enemies its passenger can hit, as armed units do, and goes on once they are gone; an empty one drives to the point. It never fires itself, and it keeps repairing other vehicles and itself throughout (CMB-16, CMB-17). An idle Mender never goes after an enemy on its own; its passenger still fires at whatever comes in reach. The AI's use of Menders (AI-29) is unchanged.
- **NET-7**: Board and unload are ordinary commands: in an online match they travel and are checked like every other command (only well-formed fields survive and `player` is the sender's seat, NET-5), the simulation refuses those for units the player does not own, and the state checksum covers carriers, passengers and what each mender mends, so both players and replays stay identical. The protocol version goes up.
- **CUR-6**: With infantry squads that can board selected, pointing at one of the player's own Field Menders that has room shows a board cursor (a green hatch with a down arrow), and right-clicking it orders those squads to board; the rest of the selection gets no order from that click. CUR-1's other cursors are unchanged.
- **CTL-22**: R unloads every selected Field Mender that carries a squad. The F1 controls overlay lists it, with right-clicking a Field Mender to board.
- **UI-23**: With one or more Field Menders selected, the selection panel shows each passenger squad (its name and figures standing), or that the Mender is empty, and an Unload button that unloads the selected Menders that carry a squad, as R does.
- **R3D-38**: The Field Mender is an Aegis utility vehicle in the grounded science-fiction style of R3D-35: six wheels in armoured hubs under a sealed, faceted body; a closed crew compartment at the rear with a roof hatch whose light glows only while a squad is inside; and a long crane arm on a swivel on the roof: a boom, a stick hung beside it and a tool head, together close to a tile long as drawn. The whole model is drawn at four fifths of its modelled size, so it stands smaller than a Bulwark tank (issue #196). At rest the arm lies folded flat on the roof; while it mends, it swings toward the vehicle it is mending, unfolds (in well under a second) and pitches its tool head down toward it, and folds back when done. Once it is out, a green-blue repair stream runs from the tool head's tip to the side of that vehicle facing it, where a continuous spray of bright welding sparks flies off, falling under gravity, some bouncing once off the ground before they fade, at a steady rate whatever the frame rate, with a hot glow on the contact point and a flickering light from the existing point-light pool (no new lights, PERF-5); a burst of sparks there marks each repair. Its parts stay clear of each other, of the hull and of the ground in every pose, folded, unfolding and out (AIM-2). While it is selected, a thin green-blue circle on the ground around it shows its repair reach, 3 tiles from its centre (CMB-16), in both views (issue #196). The unit sheet says what it does in place of what it is strong against: "Repairs vehicles within 3 tiles; carries one infantry squad" (INF-3). Shots of its passenger leave from the hatch. In the classic view it is a simple drawing: a hull with a team stripe, an arm and a hatch light when loaded.
- **AUD-17**: A Field Mender makes a welding crackle each time it repairs, and a clunk when a squad climbs in or out. Both are procedural, in the Aegis palette (AUD-6), and play like other world sounds (AUD-1, AUD-9).
- **AI-29**: The Aegis AI, on every difficulty (AI-10), keeps one Field Mender for every three tanks it has, trained at its factories (none while it plays Raider, whose army is fast and cheap: AI-14), and loads each with a Rocketeer squad it has. Its Menders follow its tanks (the middle of those still fighting). A tank of its (not its hero) below half health falls back to its nearest Mender rather than to its base, stays out of its waves while it is mended and rejoins at 90% health. It unloads a Mender's squad when the Mender drops below 15% health, or when the Mender has been out of combat (no enemy within 8 tiles) and has not moved for 10 seconds. The steamroll opponent (HAR-5) keeps Menders in its army by the same count and loads them the same way.
