# 018 Performance and security (milestone 18)

Frame-rate headroom with the least change to how the game looks or plays, and safe defaults for the website, the
desktop app and CI.

- **PERF-1**: The view does no per-frame work that cannot show: the minimap redraws its fog, buildings and shards at most five times a second (units, alert pings and the camera box every frame); units more than a couple of tiles outside the view are not posed (their walk and aim carry on, so nothing jumps when they come into view); a part's rotation is rewritten only when it changes; and the sun's shadows are redrawn every other frame.
- **PERF-2**: When frames run slow (under 50 fps) for about 1.5 s while the graphics chip is the bottleneck (the GPU timer says so when it has a reading, otherwise the CPU side of a frame is light), the 3D view renders fewer pixels, a step of 0.1 device pixels per CSS pixel at a time, down to 0.85 (or the screen's own ratio if lower); after about eight seconds of frames on time it steps back up, to at most 1.25, and when the GPU timer has a reading only if the GPU time predicted one step up stays under 13 ms. A game that holds 60 fps never loses resolution. Hidden tabs and other long pauses are ignored.
- **PERF-3**: Scrolling the camera is smooth. While the view pans steadily across the map (keys or screen edge), in a quiet match and in a 200-unit brawl, High on Intel integrated graphics shows no frame longer than 25 ms after the first second, and the ground moves the same distance on screen every frame that takes the same time. Nothing is built, compiled or uploaded for the first time because it scrolled into view, and a change of render resolution (PERF-2) does not cost a slow frame. `npm run perf3d` measures a pan as its own case.
- **PERF-4**: On High, shadows are drawn from simplified shapes: a unit or building casts its shadow from a low-detail stand-in for its silhouette and small parts cast none. The picture at the normal play zoom is not visibly different, and the instanced batches of R3D-13 are kept.
- **PERF-5**: On High, the picture is finished in two full-screen passes after the scene and its bloom: one adds bloom's glow to the scene, tone maps it and writes 8-bit colour, and FXAA then smooths the edges onto the canvas. The scene is still drawn in half-float, so dark gradients do not band; bloom keeps its threshold, strength, radius and blur levels and blurs at a sixteenth of the pixels; tone mapping and FXAA are three.js's own, so the picture looks the same as with separate bloom, tone-mapping and FXAA passes. The canvas is multisampled only for a match that starts on Low (High's FXAA quad gains nothing from it). Muzzle flashes and blasts light the scene from a pool of two point lights, made once per match, so the brightest flashes keep a light and no program is rebuilt mid-match.
- **SEC-1**: The website and the desktop app serve the game under the same strict Content-Security-Policy: scripts, workers and data only from the game's own origin, no inline scripts or `eval`, no plugins, and no framing by other sites. The website also sends `nosniff`, `X-Frame-Options: DENY`, `Referrer-Policy: no-referrer`, a Permissions-Policy that turns off the camera, microphone, location, payment and USB, and `Cross-Origin-Opener-Policy: same-origin`. The build publishes no source maps.
- **SEC-2**: The offline cache stores only complete, successful responses for the game's own files (never errors or other sites), serves them only when the network fails, and drops caches from older versions.
- **SEC-3**: Values from the address bar are validated before use: a seed is used only when it is a whole number of up to nine digits (otherwise a fresh seed is drawn), and an unknown map, faction or difficulty falls back to the default.
- **SEC-4**: CI runs with a read-only token and fails when a dependency shipped to players has a known high or critical vulnerability.
